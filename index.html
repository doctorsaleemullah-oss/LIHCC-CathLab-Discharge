This guide summarizes what to expect and how to care for yourself after your cardiac catheterization procedure. It does not replace instructions from your care team — always follow what your doctor or nurse told you at discharge.

⚠️ Call 911 immediately if you have

  • Chest pain, pressure, or tightness
  • Shortness of breath or trouble breathing
  • Sudden numbness or weakness in your face, arm, or leg, or trouble speaking
  • Bleeding at the puncture site that does not stop with firm pressure
  • Rapid swelling at the puncture site
  • A limb that becomes cold, numb, or discolored

📞 Call your care team if you have

  • A fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher
  • Redness, warmth, swelling, or drainage at the puncture site
  • A bruise that keeps getting bigger
  • Blood in your urine or black/tarry stools
  • An irregular, very slow, or very fast heartbeat
  • Dizziness or lightheadedness

🚗 The first 24 hours

  • Have a responsible adult stay with you and drive you home
  • Do not drive, operate machinery, or make important decisions
  • Do not drink alcohol
  • Rest and avoid strenuous activity
  • Drink 6–8 glasses of water to help clear the contrast dye
  • Check your temperature if you feel unwell

🩹 Wound care

Keep the groin site clean and dry. You may remove the dressing the day after your procedure and shower normally. Avoid tub baths, hot tubs, and swimming for 1 week. Avoid bending or stooping from the waist and limit stair climbing for 2–3 days.

Keep your wrist band on for the time your nurse instructed, then keep the site clean and dry. Do not bend your wrist for 24 hours. Do not soak the site for 3 days. Avoid lifting more than 3–5 lbs with that arm for 1 week.

Use the arm sling for the first 24 hours. Do not raise your arm above your shoulder for 1 week and avoid lifting more than 3–5 lbs with that arm for 1 week.

Keep the site clean and dry. Avoid lifting more than 10 lbs for 3 days and avoid turning your neck sharply.

A small bruise or a marble-sized lump is normal. Do not use lotions, powders, or ointments on the site for 1 week.

If the site starts bleeding: Lie down, apply firm direct pressure for 10–15 minutes, and call your care team. If bleeding does not stop, call 911.

🏃 Activity

  • Do only light activity for 2–3 days
  • No strenuous exercise (running, swimming, cycling, golf, weightlifting) for 1 week
  • Avoid sexual activity for 1 week
  • Climb stairs slowly and only as needed for the first 2 days
  • Ask your care team when you can return to work

💊 Medications

Take your medications exactly as listed on your after-visit summary. If you were prescribed a new blood thinner or antiplatelet medicine (such as aspirin or clopidogrel), do not stop taking it without talking to your cardiologist — stopping suddenly can increase your risk of a blood clot.

🥗 Diet & hydration

  • Drink plenty of water over the next 24 hours
  • Eat a heart-healthy diet, low in salt and saturated fat, unless told otherwise
  • If you have diabetes, resume your usual meal plan and insulin as directed
  • If you take metformin, ask your care team when it is safe to restart it

📅 Follow-up

Schedule a follow-up appointment with your cardiologist within 1–2 weeks, or as instructed at discharge. Bring your medication list and any questions about your results.

Cardiac Rehab Referral: Ask your care team whether a cardiac rehabilitation program is right for you.
